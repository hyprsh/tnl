# Outposts may declare their Routes, and the Edge keeps the Declaration

An Outpost can list its Routes in its own settings, one `TNL_ROUTE_<LABEL>` variable per Route holding the arguments of `tnl route add`, so one Compose file holds a whole setup. Whenever a digest in its 10 s ping shows that the Edge's copy differs, the Outpost sends this Declaration over the Tunnel API. The Edge saves the Declaration itself, not only the Routes it yields. A line whose Hostname is taken or outside every Zone waits there and becomes a Route by itself, in the same locked change that frees the Hostname or adds the Zone, so moving a Service between Outposts works in either order. While an Outpost declares, its Declaration is the whole truth. Applying it is a diff by Hostname, `tnl route add`, `edit` and `rm` are refused for that Outpost on both sides, and an empty Declaration removes its Routes and hands it back to the CLI. Routes are still stored and served only on the Edge ([ADR 0001](0001-outpost-holds-no-route-table.md), [ADR 0004](0004-outposts-manage-routes-over-the-tunnel.md)). We chose this over a YAML file, which would put a parser in the Outpost build and add a second syntax for two options. We also rejected storing only the applied Routes, where a migration would depend on order and a refused line would be forgotten until the next restart, and all-or-nothing applies, where one mistyped Hostname would freeze every other change. An explicit switch variable would have added one more line to every Compose file. Docker labels stay out, because reading them needs Docker's socket, which is root on the host. The costs: a second kind of thing in the Edge's state, a re-check whenever a Route is removed or a Zone added, and no way to leave declaring while keeping the Routes live. Compose files will also depend on the syntax, so it can change only at a major.

See [Routes declared on the Outpost](https://github.com/hyprsh/tnl/issues/17).

Amended by [ADR 0011](0011-the-edge-may-declare-its-zones-and-outposts.md): the Edge refuses `TNL_ROUTE_*`, and the Edge's own declared lines hand their items back to the CLI when none are set, rather than removing them as an empty Declaration does.
